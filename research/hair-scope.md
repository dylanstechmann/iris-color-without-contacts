# Scope decision: cosmetic hair-follicle pigmentation

*Recommendation recorded October 11, 2026.*

Cosmetic hair-follicle pigmentation merits a separate theoretical project, with the suggested name **hair-pigmentation-modulation**. The iris repo can share melanogenesis references with it, while each project evaluates its own tissue and outcomes.

## Why separate the projects

Hair pigmentation is integrated with follicle regeneration and the production of a growing hair fiber. A mouse lineage-tracing study showed that melanocyte stem cells move between follicle compartments and reversible differentiation states; aging can leave cells stranded and unable to contribute to pigment-cell regeneration. This provides a follicle-specific research framework, not proof of controlled human cosmetic color selection. [Sun et al., Nature, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-05960-6)

A human observational study mapped pigmentation patterns along 397 hairs from 14 healthy donors and documented rare gray/white-to-pigmented transitions within individual fibers. These occurred in scalp, pubic, and beard hairs, including patterns consistent with reversal during the same growth phase. Since shaft segments preserve a record of what the follicle produced when they formed, this supports temporary plasticity of pigment production in living follicles; it is not recoloring an emerged shaft or clearing stored pigment elsewhere. The cohort was recruited from people who self-reported some gray or two-colored hairs, and participants with two-colored hair were asked to provide all such hairs. Only 14 participants were identified over 2.5 years of active recruitment, so the study cannot estimate how common these reversals are in the general population. The proposed association with psychosocial stress was based on retrospective, case-level timing and does not establish causation or a controllable signal. [Rosenberg et al., eLife, 2021](https://elifesciences.org/articles/67437)

A complementary human mini-organ experiment found opposite-direction responses to mTORC1 modulation: TSC2 knockdown reduced pigmentation, while rapamycin increased it in some short-term organ-cultured gray/white scalp follicles. The positive response was limited to selected follicles with residual pigment cells; the study used a small donor set and was not treatment in living people. No washout or repeated switching established reversibility. [Suzuki et al., 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37212043/).

A 2025 observational report described repigmentation in 10 patients who received rose-stem-cell-derived exosomes using varied procedures. With no control group and multiple procedural approaches, it cannot isolate an exosome effect or establish a reliable, durable controller. [Lueangarun et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41201122/).

A 2026 prospective single-center cohort compared 18 patients receiving PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor-containing cancer regimens with 11 receiving chemotherapy alone. Trichoscopic grayscale darkening was greater in the immunotherapy group (mean Δ 23.5 ± 24.67 vs 4.32 ± 9.01; p=.0065), but most patients in the ICI group also received chemotherapy, treatment was not randomized, and the groups differed in tumor mix. The measure is a hair-image darkness proxy, not melanin mass; the study did not test treatment withdrawal or repeat switching. Seven of 18 ICI-group patients experienced alopecia. This is oncology-associated follicular plasticity evidence, not a cosmetic intervention. [Jiang et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42387269/).

Thus the evidence spans spontaneous within-shaft transitions, short-term ex-vivo modulation in selected human follicles, small uncontrolled reports, and a 2026 prospective but nonrandomized oncology cohort. None demonstrates a standardized, controlled, repeatable cosmetic treatment for living people.

**Inference for the proposed scope:** modulation of pigment production in a follicle would primarily be evaluated in newly formed hair segments. It would not amount to biological recoloring of an already-grown external hair shaft. This is a different endpoint from reducing pigment already resident in an adult iris.

## Distinct cosmetic questions

| Hair question | Main conceptual endpoint |
| --- | --- |
| Lightening | Reduced pigment incorporated into newly growing hair |
| Changing shade | Effects of pigment amount, pigment type, and hair-fiber optics |
| Reversing graying | Recovery of pigment production and the function of the pigment-cell niche |

The hair project should consider follicle function, hair growth, surrounding skin effects, appearance of new growth, durability, and reversibility. Eye research must assess vision and ocular function, so shared melanin biology cannot establish that a result transfers between the tissues.

The proposed hair project would cover pigmentation. Hair growth, removal, and follicle regeneration as broader cosmetic goals would need their own explicit scope.
