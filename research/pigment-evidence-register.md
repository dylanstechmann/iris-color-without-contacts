# Pigment evidence register

*Reviewed October 11, 2026. Primary studies and official clinical/regulatory sources used in the [tissue atlas](tissue-pigment-atlas.md).*

This is a scoped source register, not a systematic review or a global audit of product availability. Experimental treatment details are omitted. Clinical approvals refer to the stated condition; none of the rows establishes universal cosmetic clearance.

## Skin

Does not establish a general body-color controller. |
| [Castanedo-Cazares et al., 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23355788/) | Nine-week, randomized, double-blind axillary-hyperpigmentation study in 24 women; baseline/end biopsies with Fontana–Masson staining | Stained epidermal melanin area fell with niacinamide and desonide, but not placebo; NKI/Beteb-positive melanocyte counts did not significantly change. | [Navarrete-Solís et al., 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3142702/) | Eight-week split-face niacinamide-versus-hydroquinone trial in 27 women with melasma; post-treatment biopsy from niacinamide side in 11 participants | Fontana–Masson-positive epidermal melanin area declined from 8.7% to 6% in sampled niacinamide-treated lesions. No paired post-treatment biopsy from the hydroquinone side or placebo arm; stain area, not chemical mass/fate or withdrawal. |
| [Split-face randomized melasma trial, 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33017045/) | Human split-face randomized trial; 28 completed 12 weeks of treatment, 21 completed 12-week follow-up | Both interventions modestly reduced clinical severity/melanin index; all 21 follow-up completers had reappearance by follow-up end. Disease recurrence after treatment, not direct pigment-mass restoration or proof of clearance. |
| [Tancrède-Bohin et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25220296/) | In-vivo multiphoton time course in 20 women using an occlusive retinoid screening model; measurements at baseline, end of exposure, and 6 and 20 days later | Living-epidermis melanin density was lower on the retinol area at all three post-baseline measurements; retinoic acid showed a significant decrease only at the end-of-exposure visit. Retinol remained lower at the last visit, so no return was observed by day 32. Optical density/time course, not chemical mass, pigment export, or a controlled cosmetic cycle. |
| [Peña et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8803839/) | 3D in-vivo multiphoton Pseudo-FLIM method paper; synthetic melanin, human melanocyte/keratinocyte cocultures, and human skin cohorts | Produces a global epidermal density estimate and a 12-layer depth profile; compared with FLIM/phasor analyses and reported agreement with prior ex-vivo histology/HPLC evidence and correlation with skin color. Not a paired serial chemical assay of treatment-induced pigment loss; small optical field, no pigment-fate tracking. |
| [Chen et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33549605/) | Stable-isotope [U-13C]-L-tyrosine LC–MS fate tracing in live human and mouse melanocyte models | Quantified de novo melanin-pathway intermediates for eumelanin and pheomelanin synthesis over short timescales. The tracer labels newly synthesized products; it does not label pre-existing polymer or track tissue-to-output clearance. |
| [Lerche et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31233275/) | LC-MS/MS method study; 30 volunteers provided one 4-mm buttock biopsy each | Quantified two eumelanin markers (PTCA, PDCA) and two pheomelanin markers (TDCA, TTCA) in a single run. Cross-sectional by skin phototype; processed-sample markers, not in-vivo catabolite tracing or a treatment-related mass balance. |
| [Parsad et al., 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14510999/) | HPLC analysis of post-PUVA biopsies from depigmented and repigmented patches in five vitiligo patients | Quantified eumelanin and pheomelanin separately and detected pigment in long-standing depigmented lesions. Samples were obtained after treatment, not as a paired clearance time course; disease-specific repigmentation, not cosmetic depigmentation or pigment export. |
| [Tancrède-Bohin et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7754381/) | In-vivo 3D multiphoton study of 30 women followed during one year of usual retinoid application; post-study biopsies assessed other structural endpoints | Retinoids limited the seasonal ascent of melanin into suprabasal layers; slight global-density decrease was reported with retinoic acid. Authors interpret the distribution pattern mainly through epidermal renewal. No treatment-withdrawal recovery test or pigment-export mass balance. |
| [Ning et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2025.2790) | Single-center observational TPM study of 60 people with melasma; 53 completed 12-week treatment/follow-up | Semiquantitative epidermal melanin scores fell across all five layers and activated melanocyte scores fell. No untreated comparator in the treatment phase; five discontinued for hydroquinone allergy and two were lost. Disease-specific optical monitoring; no chemical mass balance, product fate, or reversal assessment. |
| [SCENESSE U.S. label and EMA assessment](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=94f53286-11dd-7fbb-e053-2a95a90a7c48&version=11), [EMA product information](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/scenesse) | Official U.S./EU product information; afamelanotide implant | Adult EPP indication; MC1-R agonism stimulates skin eumelanin and increases pain-free light exposure. Requires clinician-administered subcutaneous implantation. Demonstrates disease-specific skin pigmentation increase, not cosmetic shade control or reversal. |
| [Historical FDA Benoquin label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2003/08173slr015_benoquin_lbl.pdf), [FDA 2022 review](https://www.fda.gov/media/160999/download?attachment=) | Historical U.S. label and later FDA regulatory review | Labeled only for final depigmentation in extensive vitiligo; warned melanocyte destruction, frequently irreversible / distant irregular depigmentation, and lifelong skin sensitivity. FDA's 2022 review records the manufacturer's report that the product was no longer manufactured or distributed and had been de-listed. Not cosmetic clearance or reversible control. |
| [Lee et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26440747/) | In-vitro comparison in normal human epidermal melanocytes | Monobenzone concentrations that inhibited melanogenesis were similar to cytotoxic concentrations; cell model, not a treatment study. |
| [FDA ruxolitinib vitiligo approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-topical-treatment-addressing-repigmentation-vitiligo-patients-aged-12-and-older) | Official U.S. approval and clinical-trial summary | Disease-specific repigmentation; not arbitrary darkening of healthy skin. |
| [Opzelura U.S. prescribing information, revised September 2025](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/215309s007lbl.pdf) | Current official U.S. prescription label | Indicated for topical treatment of nonsegmental vitiligo in adults and patients aged 12 years and older. Disease-directed repigmentation does not establish healthy-skin shade modification, stored-pigment clearance, or return to a chosen baseline. |
| [TRuE-V randomized withdrawal study, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjd/ljaf519) | Phase III long-term extension; patients with ≥F-VASI90 after 52 weeks were randomized to 52 weeks of ruxolitinib continuation or vehicle withdrawal; 56 withdrawal and 55 continuation participants evaluable | Relapse below F-VASI75 occurred in 16/56 (29%) after withdrawal and 8/55 (15%) with continuation; Kaplan–Meier estimated one-year F-VASI75 maintenance was 65.0% vs 84.3%. Of 16 withdrawal relapsers, 12 regained F-VASI75 after rescue (median 85 days) and 11 regained F-VASI90 (median 106 days). Selected facial high responders; severity scores, not direct melanin mass, healthy-baseline return, or repeated cosmetic-cycle outcomes; attrition may bias estimates. |
| [Hakozaki et al., 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12100180/) | Cell/reconstructed epidermis experiments plus human studies | Transfer modulation in models; human facial appearance outcome. Does not prove digestion of existing pigment in people. |
| [Kim et al., 2020; first published 2019](https://doi.org/10.1111/pcmr.12838) | Cultured human melanocytes and keratinocytes containing transferred melanosomes | PTPD-12 increased autophagic flux and reduced melanosome signal/lightened cell pellets. Autophagy also affects melanocyte biology; no intact-skin or human-treatment result, and no demonstration of a reversible color cycle. |
| [Geyfman et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39305105/) | Preclinical study: normal human melanocytes, MelanoDerm skin equivalents, and human melanoma-cell mechanisms | Melanin accumulation fell alongside autophagy activation and melanosome changes; company-sponsored, with small technical replicate counts. No human treatment or iris result. |
| [Zhao et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/jocd.70770) | Normal human epidermal keratinocytes loaded with melanosome-enriched fractions from B16 mouse melanoma cells; separate cell-free Fenton model | HCP lowered cell-normalized intracellular 550-nm absorbance (n=4) after 72 h; lysosomal and hydroxyl-radical readouts support the authors' proposed proteolysis-then-oxidation mechanism. This is an optical endpoint from the cell layer: culture-medium pigment and chemically identified degradation products were not measured, so pigment fate is unresolved. No intact skin, photoprotection/function, or human intervention. Two reporting conflicts require clarification: Methods call 6.25–25 mg/mL the final HCP concentrations while describing 1 μL added to 99 μL, whereas results/figures report 0.125–0.25 mg/mL; abstract/results associate stronger hydroxyl-radical signal and lower absorbance with alkaline conditions, while discussion reverses the pH direction. The hydroxyl-radical probe only partly overlapped a lysosomal marker. Osman Biological Co. funded the study; author affiliations include Osman Biological and Beauty Hi-Tech, although the authors declared no conflicts. |
| [Mokrzyński et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38803190/) | Synthetic DOPA-melanin subjected to oxidative photolysis or hydrogen-peroxide exposure | Oxidative bleaching changed redox/antioxidant properties and increased singlet-oxygen photogeneration; synthetic pigment model, not a clearance intervention in human tissue. |
| [Cabaço et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9460155/) | Primary human neonatal melanocytes and keratinocytes in co-culture; conditioned-medium and RAB3A/RAB11B perturbation experiments | Medium from differentiated primary human keratinocytes stimulated RAB3A-dependent melanin exocytosis/transfer. Exocytosed melanin and recipient keratinocyte pigment compartments were quantified in culture; no intact-tissue flux, recipient-cell degradation, or external mass balance. |
| [Ando et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7461560/) | Normal human melanocytes, dermal fibroblasts, and macrophages in culture; secretory clusters isolated from melanocyte medium | Phosphatidylserine-exposed melanosome clusters were internalized by human fibroblasts and macrophages; fibroblast melanin per cell increased through 48 h. Fibroblasts also internalized apoptotic melanocytes. The proposed basement-membrane route was not directly tested in intact skin; uptake is not proof of pigment breakdown or tissue exit. |
| [Hada et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/pcmr.70110) | Cross-sectional histopathology of 14 human ashy-dermatosis biopsies; autophagy markers in a subset (7 cases, 5 healthy controls) | Fibroblasts accounted for a significantly greater share of melanosome-containing cells than macrophages in most cases (12/14, p=.0049). Macrophage p62 was higher in the subset (p=.0101), consistent with impaired autophagy. Static disease pathology measures cell-type distribution, not uptake rate, pigment degradation, or clearance. |
| [Ebanks et al., 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21326292/) | Cultured human keratinocytes from light- and dark-skin donors | Transferred-melanosome loss differed by donor source; cell model, not a selectable treatment. |
| [Murase et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1038/jid.2013.165) | Human keratinocytes, human skin substitutes, and ex-vivo cultured human skin | Activators lowered measured melanin; autophagy/lysosome inhibitors raised it. This is two-direction responsiveness in models, not a demonstrated washout reversal or a treatment in living people. |
| [Benito-Martínez et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41398050/) | Human keratinocyte model internalizing extracellular melanin; KRT5/14, microtubule, and plectin-1 perturbation | KRT5/14 intermediate filaments and microtubules maintain supranuclear 3D positioning of melanin organelles; positioning protected DNA from UVB in the model. Spatial-function result, not total pigment reduction, degradation, or export. Several authors were L’Oréal R&I employees; the company provided research support. |
| [Murase et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7460956/) | Human hyperpigmented skin observations plus ex-vivo elbow-skin culture from four donors | Five-day autophagy restoration reduced melanin-positive area and epidermal thickness while improving epidermal-differentiation marker patterns. Ex-vivo disease model; no living-person treatment, identified melanin products, export balance, or washout/return test. |
| [Homma et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29654760/) | Human normal/hyperpigmented skin immunohistochemistry plus CTSV-knockdown cells | Basal-layer CTSV signal was lower in a hyperpigmented human specimen; CTSV knockdown suppressed cellular melanosome degradation. Small biopsy comparison and cell mechanism, not a treatment result. |
| [Correia et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29074272/) | Human melanocore uptake assay in cultured keratinocytes | Melanin localized to hybrid endocytic compartments that were not highly acidic or degradative; model-specific persistence. |
| [Hurbain et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29054596/) | Electron microscopy and tomography of human skin in situ across pigmentation phenotypes | Some keratinocyte melanocore clusters were nonacidic and nondegradative; storage context matters. |
| [Neto et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jid.2024.08.023) | Mechanistic experiments mainly in murine XB2 keratinocytes fed melanocores from human MNT1 melanoma cells; reconstructed human pigmented epidermis examined by TEM/volume TEM | Melanin-containing compartments acquired LAMP1/CD63 and fused with lysosomes; cathepsin-B activity fell after 24 hours and mature compartments became less accessible to fluid-phase cargo by 72 hours. Authors propose a weakly degradative storage lysosome (“melanokerasome”). No direct chemical measure of melanin-core loss, export, or living-human intervention; the human-equivalent model supplied ultrastructural confirmation. |
| [Joly-Tonetti et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjd.16926) | Histology and high-resolution microscopy of adult human epidermis at rest and after a regenerative stimulus; in-vitro uptake models also attempted | Proposed asymmetric melanin-organelle inheritance in basal keratinocyte mitoses, with more symmetric distribution under regenerative stress. Preliminary distribution model; does not track pigment mass, prove no degradation, or establish net export. |
| [Kobayashi et al., 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9579550/) | Human skin explants | Supranuclear melanin caps correlated with fewer UV-induced DNA photoproducts; native protective function is relevant. |
| [Yi et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6056223/) | Human melanocores and cultured keratinocyte model | CTSV-related melanocore degradation reduced antioxidant/photoprotective function; UVA/UVB exposure also suppressed CTSV in cultured keratinocytes. Supports function as a separate endpoint; not an intervention trial. |
| [PNAS melanophagy study, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998605/) | Mainly B16F10 mouse melanoma cells; secondary transfer comparison in HaCaT keratinocytes | RCHY1/OPTN/TBK1-linked melanophagy reduced melanosome and intracellular melanin measures in B16F10 cells. The β-mangostin treatment did not reduce melanin in HaCaT keratinocytes after uptake of B16F10-derived melanosomes. Model-dependent evidence; no intact skin or human intervention. |
| [Park et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1080/15548627.2024.2421695) | TCTE-induced melanophagy experiments mainly in B16F1 mouse melanoma cells; supplemental validation in Melan-A cells; PTK2/ITCH/MLANA/OPTN perturbations | Proposed PTK2–ITCH–MLANA–OPTN cascade; ITCH or OPTN knockdown attenuated the model's depigmentation response. Cell-model mechanism only; no human skin treatment, in-vivo pigment fate, or washout/reversal endpoint. |
| [Saurav et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3003971) | Mechanistic melanophagy assays in B16 mouse melanoma cells, primary lightly pigmented human melanocytes, and zebrafish embryos; live-cell reporters, biochemical and ultrastructural assays, and IP3R2 knockdown/rescue | IP3R2 silencing enhanced melanophagy. Primary human melanocytes showed lower pellet-image pigmentation intensity (not chemical melanin mass); embryo-stage zebrafish showed ~35% lower melanin at 48 hpf, with rescue. Mechanism implicates AMPK–ULK1 and lysosomal Ca2+–TRPML1–TFEB signaling to RCHY1/OPTN. No adult-skin intervention, local selectivity, safety/function, pigment-product/export balance, or washout-return test. |
| [Hwang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00018-026-06389-6) | Paired human solar-lentigo lesional/non-lesional skin plus MNT-1 pigmented melanoma and HaCaT keratinocyte models with sustained IRE1α expression; verapamil and STF083010 were tested in cell models | Lesional tissue showed dense stage-IV melanosomes with increased IRE1α and p16. In models, IRE1α increased melanin production in MNT-1 cells and slowed melanosome degradation in HaCaT cells; interventions partly restored degradation and reduced accumulation in vitro. Accepted early-access disease-mechanism study, not a human clearance intervention; no chemical pigment-product/export balance, normal-skin function, or washout-return endpoint. |
| [Pospíšil et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41088527/) | UV-exposed ex-vivo porcine skin; EPR spin-trapping of organic radicals and HPLC-MS analysis of short-chain carbonyl species | Authors propose lipid-peroxidation-derived triplet carbonyls transfer energy to melanin, generating secondary excited states and possible post-exposure oxidative stress. No measured melanin-mass decrease, tissue export, DNA-injury endpoint, or living-human result; safety-mechanism evidence, not clearance. |
| [Marubashi and Fukuda, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10739166/) | Mouse keratinocyte model with transferred melanosomes | Rab7B/42-dependent degradation of melanosomal proteins; measured melanin remained unchanged even after two weeks. Strong endpoint distinction, not a human treatment. |
| [Otaki and Seiji, 1971](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5556529/) | Classic isolated-organelle lysosomal-digestion study | Lysosomal processing of melanosomes distinguished degradable organelle constituents from the resistant melanin core; cell-free assay, not tissue clearance. |
| [Park et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11010-015-2645-2) | Abstract-level report of lysosomal/peroxisomal activation and melanin-associated color reduction in HeLa/mammalian-cell models | Reports lower color intensity/amount following organelle activity; available abstract does not establish direct polymer cleavage, chemical products, or export. |
| [Park et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34346036/) | Cell-free fungal laccase study using synthetic and natural human-hair melanin | Enzyme preparation decolorized isolated pigment; does not show intracellular delivery, human tissue selectivity, product safety, or clearance. |
| [FDA tattoo removal](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/tattoo-removal-options-and-results) | Official U.S. device/consumer information | Cleared laser devices; no approved removal creams. Exogenous dermal pigment is distinct from native melanin. |
| [Taylor et al., 1991](https://doi.org/10.1111/1523-1747.ep12478570) | Human tattoo biopsies examined by light and electron microscopy before and after Q-switched ruby laser exposure | Pigment-containing cells were disrupted, particles fragmented, and inflammation observed; pigment was again intracellular at 11 days. Local fading does not establish whole-body elimination. |
| [Dujmović et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32650854/) | Human case report; regional-node pathology after tattoo removal by dermabrasion | Tattoo pigment remained visible in regional lymph nodes three years later; demonstrates persistence/redistribution after a different procedure, not laser clearance. |
| [Schreiver et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-11721-z) | Postmortem human tattooed skin and regional lymph-node samples; synchrotron XRF and mass spectrometry | Matching tattoo pigments were detected in skin and nodes in 2 of 4 tattooed donors; small distribution study, not post-laser fate or body elimination. |
| [Baranska et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1084/jem.20171608) | Mouse CD64-dependent macrophage-depletion, parabiotic, graft, and tattoo-persistence models | Dermal macrophages captured and retained tattoo ink; after macrophage turnover, ink was recaptured and the tattoo persisted through 90 days. The study states mouse dermal macrophages did not migrate to draining nodes under normal or inflammatory conditions; laser-fragmented ink may enter lymphatics. This is exogenous-ink mechanism evidence, not native melanin fate or whole-body elimination. |
| [O'Malley et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41918289/) | Histopathologic review of 90 lymph nodes with tattoo-ink deposition | No unifying histologic pattern beyond the ink; 11 cases had hematologic neoplasms. Selected pathology specimens cannot estimate prevalence or establish a causal association. |
| [Fukayama et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9309704/) | Single generalized-argyria case with pre/post skin biopsies, electron microscopy, and mass spectrometry | Treated skin showed fragmented silver-containing deposits and lower silver signal; silver was also found in macrophages and residual deposits. Long-term appearance improvement; authors infer partial clearance, but no body-level balance. |
| [Krase et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28296792/) | Human case report of argyria recurrence after Q-switched laser treatment | Documents recurrence; a single report does not identify the mechanism or establish total-silver change. |
| [Maeda, 2017](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9284/4/4/47) | Small human study, six men; inner forearm | Fading of UVA-induced pigmentation used as an epidermal-turnover proxy (36.2 ± 6.2 days); not a direct mass balance for all stored melanin or selective clearance. |
| [Man et al., 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4134407/) | Human darkly pigmented epidermis examined alongside pigmented/nonpigmented mouse models and organotypic human keratinocyte cultures | Human tissue showed substantial melanin extrusion in the outer epidermis; the detailed perturbation/function experiments were mainly in mice. Histologic localization does not quantify natural pigment export across the skin surface. |
| [Lu et al., 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8881670/) | Human surface corneocytes from exposed and unexposed skin; image analysis | Melanin granules were measured in surface corneocytes and varied with phototype and exposure; documents a superficial pigment pool, not selective removal from the full epidermis. |
| [Matsunaka et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/srt.12309) | Human tape-sampled outer stratum corneum; HPLC; 10 healthy subjects plus a four-person time course after a single 2-MED UV exposure | Chemically quantified eumelanin and pheomelanin in surface samples; the 20-week same-site time course reported a melanin peak at weeks 11–15. This is a slowly evolving surface-pool signal, not whole-tissue mass balance, exported mass, or a clearance intervention. |
| [Joly-Tonetti et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/exd.13016) | Comparative histology study using photo-protected human skin from 12 donors, phototypes I–VI; Warthin–Starry, Fontana–Masson and Von Kossa stains compared with TEM granule counts; also reassessed prior literature | Warthin–Starry counts tracked TEM more closely; Fontana–Masson/Von Kossa undercounted suprabasal granules, and diffuse Fontana–Masson stratum-corneum darkening appeared where granules were absent, supporting a nonspecific silver-deposition artifact. The stain comparison does not measure pigment turnover or export. |
| [Yakimov et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-71220-6) | In-vivo depth-resolved confocal Raman/fluorescence study of 10 healthy volunteers, skin phototypes II–III | Melanin-associated Raman bands were separated at epidermal depths, with signal in some stratum-corneum spectra. Small cohort and spectral inference; does not identify “dust” particle morphology, degradation products, or source-to-surface flux. |
| [Goncalves et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/btm2.10415) | Pigmented full-thickness human skin-equivalent model; tape stripping of the stratum corneum | The model lightened and measured tissue melanin staining/index fell after ten tape strips; TEWL increased, indicating barrier impairment. No analysis of pigment on the strips, human intervention, or whole-body mass balance. |
| [Ito et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms27093973) | Ex-vivo human surgical skin, 18 specimens; UVA1 exposure; CIELAB colorimetry, spectrophotometry, and HPLC melanin markers | Visible darkening accompanied marker changes interpreted as eumelanin photodegradation/cross-linking and pheomelanin oxidation. Not a net export or clearance measurement and not a living-person intervention; authors included industry employees and a consultant. |
| [Choi et al., 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20147966/) | Human in-situ comparison, six subjects with phototypes II–III | Similar visible tanning across exposure conditions did not correspond to the same melanin response; UVA alone produced no significant melanin increase, whereas UVB-containing conditions did. Appearance is not a direct pigment-mass endpoint. |
| [Ito et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7404343/) | HPLC method validation using synthetic melanins, animal/human hair, and human epidermis | Chemically generated marker compounds quantify melanin amount/type in harvested samples; assay-generated products are not evidence of in-vivo catabolites or pigment export. |
| [One-year nitisinone pilot in OCA-1B, 2019](https://insight.jci.org/articles/view/124387) | Five adults with molecularly confirmed OCA-1B; 12 months of systemic treatment plus safety follow-up | Inner-bicep melanin index increased by 1.72 points at month 12 (95% CI 0.03–3.41; p=.047); only one of five had a qualitative visible skin change. Outer-forearm change was not statistically significant. Disease-specific, small, systemic pilot; not a cosmetic skin-control or reversal study. |

| [Kim et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules27020454) | UV-B-induced skin-hyperpigmentation mouse model; RF exposure with follow-up through 28 days while UV-B continued | LYVE-1/VEGF-C lymphatic signals rose and tyrosinase activity fell; later skin Fontana–Masson staining and melanin-containing macrophage signals were lower. Results text/Fig. 4 report increased lymph-node Fontana–Masson staining after RF, while the abstract reports decreased node melanin. Reduced synthesis confounds an export interpretation. No skin-to-node tracer, node exit, or whole-body mass balance; not human evidence or a noninvasive cosmetic method. |

| [Takematsu and Seiji, 1984](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00511063) | Dual-labeled mouse-melanoma melanosomes injected intracutaneously into guinea-pigs; macrophage-toxic carrageenan and contact-dermatitis infiltrates | Delayed-type inflammatory infiltrates were associated with increased elimination of the 3H-labeled melanin moiety from dermis; carrageenan inhibited degradation/elimination of the 14C-labeled protein moiety. Direct tracer evidence for changed local pigment fate in an animal model, not normal native human pigment. The abstract does not report destination, products, or whole-body balance; no safe, selectable control was tested. |
| [Kim et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules27020454) | UV-B-induced skin-hyperpigmentation mouse model; RF exposure with follow-up through 28 days while UV-B continued | LYVE-1/VEGF-C lymphatic signals rose and tyrosinase activity fell; later skin Fontana–Masson staining and melanin-containing macrophage signals were lower. Results text/Fig. 4 report increased lymph-node Fontana–Masson staining after RF, while the abstract reports decreased node melanin. Reduced synthesis confounds an export interpretation. No skin-to-node tracer, node exit, or whole-body mass balance; not human evidence or a noninvasive cosmetic method. |
## Gums, oral mucosa, and teeth

| Source | Evidence type / model | Endpoint and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Luo et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40751276/) | Comparative single-cell transcriptomic atlas integrating 34,969 human and mouse oral-mucosa cells | Authors identified melanocytes among human-specific populations in the analyzed datasets. This is cell-identity evidence, not a chemical melanin-mass, shed-pigment, flux, treatment, or clearance assay; it does not establish that all mouse oral sites lack melanocytes. |
| [Matuck et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpblue.2026.100007) | Integrated human adult oral-cavity atlas; >250,000 single-cell transcriptomes and >4 million spatially resolved cells across 13 niches | Melanocytes are annotated in the integrated human atlas. Cell atlas, not a pigment-burden or clearance study; it does not test a matched human–mouse comparison. |

### Anterior iris and uveal melanocytes

The uveal-cell literature supports modulation of new synthesis in preclinical systems. Human morphometry links drug-associated darkening with larger anterior-border melanin granules, without a demonstrated increase in granule counts. Serial iris-photo density measures also moved in both directions in one small treated cohort, but were not pigment assays. Human iris ultrastructure shows turnover-like morphology and clump cells that can engulf free granules, but this evidence does not quantify net pigment loss or follow its destination. Longitudinal natural-history studies show that some people have iris-color grade shifts after childhood, including lightening in a subset of adult women, but they did not quantify melanin or follow pigment fate. Aged human iris specimens show melanosome complexes, with clinical significance unresolved. The rabbit study is suggestive for suppression during an induced response, but its small groups, internal numeric discrepancy, and missing baseline/washout measurements prevent a claim of established-pigment clearance or reversible human color change.

## Unassessed compartments

Evidence summaries now cover cochlear and meningeal melanin, cardiac melanocyte-like cells, esophageal melanocytosis, nasal and laryngeal mucosa, vulvar/cervical/anorectal findings, muscle lipofuscin, adipose carotenoids, candidate melanocyte-lineage signals in organ samples, cultured adipose-derived melanocyte progenitors, bone-associated iron in overload, tendon ochronosis, pancreatic beta-cell lipofuscin, a single proximal-tubule lipofuscin finding, splenic iron stores in secondary overload, particulate anthracosis in airways and draining lymph nodes, and colon melanosis-coli natural history. These are distinct deposits with different evidence levels; the colon reports show cause-associated endoscopic appearance resolution but do not measure pigment mass or export. None establishes a general tissue-selective cosmetic clearance capability. Dedicated assessment remains for many tissues and mucosal sites. A source in one tissue does not fill the gaps. Future entries should state the chemical substrate, cell type, model, clinical indication, actual pigment endpoint, functional endpoint, and reversibility evidence.
