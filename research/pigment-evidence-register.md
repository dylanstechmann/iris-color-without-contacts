# Pigment evidence register

*Reviewed October 11, 2026. Primary studies and official clinical/regulatory sources used in the [tissue atlas](tissue-pigment-atlas.md).*

This is a scoped source register, not a systematic review or a global audit of product availability. Experimental treatment details are omitted. Clinical approvals refer to the stated condition; none of the rows establishes universal cosmetic clearance.

## Skin

| Source | Evidence type / model | Endpoint and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [TRI-LUMA prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=a35fa709-5eb5-4429-b38f-f1e0019bf0ee) | Official U.S. prescription label | Short-term moderate-to-severe facial melasma; hydroquinone identified as synthesis inhibitor. Does not establish a general body-color controller. |
| [SCENESSE U.S. label and EMA assessment](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=94f53286-11dd-7fbb-e053-2a95a90a7c48&version=11), [EMA product information](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/scenesse) | Official U.S./EU product information; afamelanotide implant | Adult EPP indication; MC1-R agonism stimulates skin eumelanin and increases pain-free light exposure. Requires clinician-administered subcutaneous implantation. Demonstrates disease-specific skin pigmentation increase, not cosmetic shade control or reversal. |
| [FDA ruxolitinib vitiligo approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-topical-treatment-addressing-repigmentation-vitiligo-patients-aged-12-and-older) | Official U.S. approval and clinical-trial summary | Disease-specific repigmentation; not arbitrary darkening of healthy skin. |
| [Hakozaki et al., 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12100180/) | Cell/reconstructed epidermis experiments plus human studies | Transfer modulation in models; human facial appearance outcome. Does not prove digestion of existing pigment in people. |
| [Kim et al., 2020; first published 2019](https://doi.org/10.1111/pcmr.12838) | Cultured human melanocytes and melanosome-containing keratinocytes | Melanosome degradation and lighter cell pellets; no clinical iris or body-wide result. |
| [Geyfman et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39305105/) | Preclinical study: normal human melanocytes, MelanoDerm skin equivalents, and human melanoma-cell mechanisms | Melanin accumulation fell alongside autophagy activation and melanosome changes; company-sponsored, with small technical replicate counts. No human treatment or iris result. |
| [Hada et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42410740/) | Histopathology of 14 human ashy-dermatosis biopsies; autophagy markers in subset n=7 | Fibroblasts showed greater melanosome uptake than macrophages; macrophage p62 suggested impaired degradation. Disease-specific observational pathology, not an intervention or proof of clearance. |
| [Ebanks et al., 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21326292/) | Cultured human keratinocytes from light- and dark-skin donors | Transferred-melanosome loss differed by donor source; cell model, not a selectable treatment. |
| [Murase et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1038/jid.2013.165) | Human keratinocytes, cultured ex-vivo human skin, and skin substitutes | Autophagy manipulation changed measured melanin in models; no controlled cosmetic intervention in people. |
| [Correia et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29074272/) | Human melanocore uptake assay in cultured keratinocytes | Melanin localized to hybrid endocytic compartments that were not highly acidic or degradative; model-specific persistence. |
| [Hurbain et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29054596/) | Electron microscopy and tomography of human skin in situ across pigmentation phenotypes | Some keratinocyte melanocore clusters were nonacidic and nondegradative; storage context matters. |
| [Kobayashi et al., 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9579550/) | Human skin explants | Supranuclear melanin caps correlated with fewer UV-induced DNA photoproducts; native protective function is relevant. |
| [Yi et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6056223/) | Physically disrupted human melanocores and cultured keratinocyte model | Intact-core structure supported antioxidant and photoprotective properties; disruption model, not an intervention trial. |
| [Melanophagy study, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10998605/) | Mainly mouse B16F10 melanoma-cell model; transferred-melanosome keratinocyte comparison | Melanophagy machinery; compartment-dependent findings. Does not establish a human treatment. |
| [Marubashi and Fukuda, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10739166/) | Mouse melanocyte/keratinocyte models | Melanosomal protein degradation without altered melanin content; endpoint distinction. |
| [FDA tattoo removal](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/tattoo-removal-options-and-results) | Official U.S. device/consumer information | Cleared laser devices; no approved removal creams. Exogenous dermal pigment is distinct from native melanin. |
| [Taylor et al., 1991](https://doi.org/10.1111/1523-1747.ep12478570) | Human tattoo biopsies examined by light and electron microscopy before and after Q-switched ruby laser exposure | Pigment-containing cells were disrupted, particles fragmented, and inflammation observed; pigment was again intracellular at 11 days. Local fading does not establish whole-body elimination. |
| [Dujmović et al., 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32650854/) | Human case report; regional-node pathology after tattoo removal by dermabrasion | Tattoo pigment remained visible in regional lymph nodes three years later; demonstrates persistence/redistribution after a different procedure, not laser clearance. |
| [Schreiver et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-11721-z) | Postmortem human tattooed skin and regional lymph-node samples; synchrotron XRF and mass spectrometry | Matching tattoo pigments were detected in skin and nodes in 2 of 4 tattooed donors; small distribution study, not post-laser fate or body elimination. |
| [O'Malley et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41918289/) | Histopathologic review of 90 lymph nodes with tattoo-ink deposition | No unifying histologic pattern beyond the ink; 11 cases had hematologic neoplasms. Selected pathology specimens cannot estimate prevalence or establish a causal association. |
| [Fukayama et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9309704/) | Single generalized-argyria case with pre/post skin biopsies, electron microscopy, and mass spectrometry | Treated skin showed fragmented silver-containing deposits and lower silver signal; silver was also found in macrophages and residual deposits. Long-term appearance improvement; authors infer partial clearance, but no body-level balance. |
| [Krase et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28296792/) | Human case report of argyria recurrence after Q-switched laser treatment | Documents recurrence; a single report does not identify the mechanism or establish total-silver change. |
| [Maeda, 2017](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9284/4/4/47) | Small human study, six men; inner forearm | Fading of UVA-induced pigmentation used as an epidermal-turnover proxy (36.2 ± 6.2 days); not a direct mass balance for all stored melanin or selective clearance. |
| [Lu et al., 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8881670/) | Human surface corneocytes from exposed and unexposed skin; image analysis | Melanin granules were measured in surface corneocytes and varied with phototype and exposure; documents a superficial pigment pool, not selective removal from the full epidermis. |
| [Matsunaka et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/srt.12309) | Human tape-sampled outer stratum corneum; HPLC; 10 healthy subjects plus a four-person time course | Chemically quantified eumelanin and pheomelanin in surface samples over time; a surface-pool measurement, not whole-tissue mass balance or a clearance intervention. |
| [Choi et al., 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20147966/) | Human in-situ comparison, six subjects with phototypes II–III | Similar visible tanning across exposure conditions did not correspond to the same melanin response; UVA alone produced no significant melanin increase, whereas UVB-containing conditions did. Appearance is not a direct pigment-mass endpoint. |
| [Ito et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7404343/) | HPLC method validation using synthetic melanins, animal/human hair, and human epidermis | Chemically generated marker compounds quantify melanin amount/type in harvested samples; assay-generated products are not evidence of in-vivo catabolites or pigment export. |

## Gums, oral mucosa, and teeth

| Source | Evidence type / model | Endpoint and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [El-Mofty et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33966113/) | Randomized human comparison, 20 participants; topical gel versus injections | Clinical index and histological stain endpoints differ; small study with no placebo arm. |
| [Gudur et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42147661/) | Randomized human trial, 20 participants; topical ascorbyl glucoside after diode-laser depigmentation versus laser alone | Nine-month clinical pigmentation index favored the adjunct arm; both groups received laser. Small procedural-adjunct study; no pigment-mass or export measurement. |
| [Scalpel/diode-laser trial, 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31983845/) | Human split-mouth scalpel/diode-laser trial, 20 participants | Procedural depigmentation, healing and recurrence; not noninvasive modulation. |
| [Oral pigmented-lesion series](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8141314/) | Retrospective human pathology series | Melanocytic and nonmelanocytic diagnoses; biopsy selection cannot estimate population prevalence. |
| [NIDCR gum disease](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov/health-info/gum-disease) | Official health information | Inflammatory gum signs belong to a separate cause branch. |
| [ADA whitening](https://www.ada.org/resources/ada-library/oral-health-topics/whitening) | Official professional clinical information | Dental stains/chromogens and whitening; not evidence for living-tissue melanin clearance. |
| [Thomson et al., 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10577752/) | In-vitro labeling of 105 samples of clinically normal human oral mucosa from several sites | S-phase labeling varied by oral site; measures proliferation, not full epithelial transit or melanin fate. |
| [Thomson et al., 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11684035/) | In-vitro labeling of 110 human mandibular gingiva and buccal mucosa samples | Buccal mucosa had a higher S-phase labeling index than mandibular gingiva; this is not a complete turnover-time or pigment-export measurement. |
| [Squier and Waterhouse, 1967](https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-9969(67)90148-3) | Light and electron microscopy of 60 normal human gingival specimens | Melanin granules were observed in gingival keratinocytes; does not quantify melanin in exfoliated cells or establish clearance. |
| [Barrett and Beynon, 1991](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1747076/) | Histochemical analysis of six oral mucosal sites in 95 human autopsies | Melanin detection varied by site; 24.2% had no detectable melanin at any sampled oral site. Establishes baseline heterogeneity, not pigment export or clearance. |

## Colon mucosa

| Source | Evidence type / model | Endpoint and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Human melanosis-coli histopathology study, 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4581826/) | Human colonic biopsies; histochemistry, immunohistochemistry, and apoptosis analysis | Identified macrophage deposits as lipofuscin and observed apoptotic bodies; does not show pigment export or whole-body clearance. The name “melanosis” is not evidence of melanin. |
| [Development, disappearance, and clinical course, 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36849146/) | Retrospective single-institution colonoscopy review of 216 cases | Ten disappearance cases were identified; 4 of those 10 had stopped anthranoids before disappearance was detected. Observational appearance endpoint, not a pigment-mass or export measurement. |
| [Colonoscopic resolution after senna cessation, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11402352/) | Human single-patient report; serial colonoscopies | Endoscopic pigmentation was no longer visible seven months after senna cessation; no follow-up biopsy to determine pigment burden or destination. |

## Eyes

| Source | Evidence type / model | Endpoint and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Imesch et al., 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8602782/) | Histology of 51 human irides | Stromal melanocyte counts/density did not significantly differ among blue, hazel, and brown groups; cell number is not the main color variable. |
| [Prota et al., 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9778410/) | Human iris microdissection and pigment chemistry | Stromal and posterior epithelial pigment profiles differ; blue irides had very low measured pigment, and cultured cells changed composition with growth/senescence. |
| [UK Xalatan SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/1616/smpc) | Official product information | Susceptibility to drug-associated darkening differs by starting color; cited uniformly blue group had no observed change. |
| [U.S. Xalatan label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f4e73059-5ba0-4d73-9ea1-09d8d654e844) | Official U.S. prescribing information | Increased iris pigment may persist; stopping the drug is not an established reversal method. |
| [Wistrand et al., 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9154289/) | Clinical latanoprost pigmentation reports with post-cessation follow-up | Induced iris pigmentation showed no observed change in patients followed for two years after stopping; report does not test active pigment removal. |
| [Camras et al., 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10708238/) | Single-patient case report; serial iris photographs for five years after discontinuation | No appreciable fading in this patient; one case cannot define all response trajectories, but documents persistence over long follow-up. |
| [Wand et al., 2001](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaophthalmology/fullarticle/265847) | Single-patient periocular-skin case report (adjacent-tissue comparator) | Skin darkening improved within one month and was significantly lighter by two months after discontinuation; not evidence of iris reversal. |
| [FDA eye-drop information](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/buying-using-medicine-safely/what-you-should-know-about-eye-drops) | Official U.S. regulatory information | No approved eye-color-changing drops. |
| [Ning et al., 2021/2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33449587/) | Human case report after cosmetic iris laser treatment | Bilateral pigment dispersion; case report establishes a possible adverse event, not incidence. |
| [Swampillai et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36175318/) | Human case report after cosmetic iris laser treatment | Secondary pigmentary glaucoma; safety data for the procedure are lacking. |
| [Liu et al., 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37771388/) | Human case report after four cosmetic laser sessions | Severe bilateral pigmentary glaucoma, very high intraocular pressure, maculopathy, and lasting visual-field/glare symptoms at follow-up. Shows possible harm, not event frequency; procedure is outside a nonsurgical selective-modulation scope. |
| [Pfeiffer et al., 2001 (Mainz I)](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaophthalmology/fullarticle/265260) | Human adult-eye and iris immunohistochemistry with PCNA and Ki-67 | No positive nuclei were observed in examined adult iris samples; a histological snapshot does not establish lifetime turnover or pigment export. |
| [Impact of pigment dispersion on trabecular meshwork cells, 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7847180/) | Primary porcine trabecular-meshwork cell model | Pigment exposure reduced phagocytosis and migration and increased cell contraction; not a human cosmetic-clearance study. |
| [IOP elevation precedes phagocytosis decline, 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5915754/) | Porcine ex-vivo pigment-dispersion model | Pigment exposure reduced outflow and raised pressure before phagocytosis declined; effects are model-specific. |
| [Zhou et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34397796/) | Human 15-year follow-up of 21 eyes in 11 patients with pigment-dispersion disease after clinical treatment | Trabecular-meshwork pigmentation attenuated over time; small treated disease cohort, not evidence for cosmetic iris clearance. |
| [Nociari et al., 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3986126/) | RPE cell and mouse models | Selected bisretinoid reduction; does not establish removal of every lipofuscin component. |
| [Lyu et al., 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10194005/) | Mouse RPE and eye-explant research | Lipofuscin degradation mechanism involving melanin; model-specific. |
| [Pfeffer comment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10622903/) and [authors' reply](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10622899/) | Scientific correspondence | Limits and alternative pathways concerning a universal requirement for melanin. No new clinical approval. |
| [Seagle et al., 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1157035/) | Human fetal RPE cultures | Melanin photochemistry/aggregation and light-related apoptosis; function must be considered. |
| [Human choroid mapping study, 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6901595/) | Human postmortem tissue and cultures | Distribution and chemical signatures; no cosmetic clearing intervention established. |
| [NLM: pigment epithelium of eye](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/mesh/D010857) | Official anatomical vocabulary | Separate iris, ciliary and retinal epithelial compartments. |
| [Postburn conjunctival pigmentation report, 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4251888/) | Human case report | Procedural photoablation; not a general reversible modulator. |

## Systemic and secondary pigmentation

| Source | Evidence type / model | Endpoint and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Berk et al., 1969](https://www.jci.org/articles/view/106184) | Human tracer study; 13 healthy adults injected with unconjugated [14C]bilirubin | Mean 96% cumulative recovery of injected radioactivity in feces and urine over 7 days. Defines a bilirubin-specific whole-body tracer endpoint; does not establish clearance of melanin, lipofuscin, or any other stored pigment. |
| [Ritter et al., 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10421657/) | Human donor livers and corresponding primary hepatocyte cultures | UGT1A1 protein correlated with liver bilirubin-glucuronidating activity; donor levels varied substantially. Supports a specialized hepatic route, not a cosmetic modulator or transferable pigment-clearance mechanism. |
| [Multi-tissue minocycline case](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9512163/) | Human ophthalmic case report | Skin, sclera, teeth, palate, ears and nailbeds in one patient; mechanism and course after cessation unclear. |
| [Skin, gum, sclera and nail case](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36158857/) | Human case report | Multiple visibly affected tissues; case report, not predictable across people. |
| [Skin and nail tissue analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3158285/) | Human histology and elemental analysis | Deposits included iron and calcium; one medication can produce different tissue signatures. |
| [Scleral pigmentation report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9186432/) | Human case series and spontaneous reports | Some minocycline-associated scleral deposits may resolve over years; others may persist. |
| [NIDDK porphyria overview](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/liver-disease/porphyria) | Official disease information | Porphyrin/precursor buildup can occur in liver or bone marrow with effects in skin and/or nervous system. |
| [Argyria laser review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25845405/) | Review of six human case reports/series | Selected skin-laser reports; not evidence of noninvasive systemic silver clearance. |

## Internal tissues and other pigment classes

| Source | Evidence type / model | Endpoint and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Levin et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19855129/) | Human pulmonary-vein/atrial tissue plus mouse cardiac-cell and genetic models | DCT-positive melanocyte-like cells identified in human heart regions; mouse cardiac melanosomes and pigment shown. DCT/cell-loss models affected arrhythmia susceptibility, but do not prove pigment amount causes human arrhythmia. No clearance endpoint. |
| [Goldgeier et al., 1984](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6699426/) | Histochemical and ultrastructural survey of 15 human brains; cell density measured in subset n=5 | Melanin-containing dendritic cells were melanocytes, concentrated over the ventrolateral medulla; limited anatomic sample, no clearance or function endpoint. No pigmented cells were observed in sampled pleural/peritoneal specimens. |
| [Gebarski and Blaivas, 1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8770250/) | Human normal-anatomy MRI/histopathology comparison and imaging cohort | T2 hypointensity corresponded to normal leptomeningeal melanin; imaging finding can mimic disease. Does not demonstrate clearance. |
| [Sun et al., 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25166018/) | Quantitative histology of 46 human temporal bones; 19 African-American and 27 Caucasian donor specimens | Pigment in stria vascularis and Rosenthal's canal differed between sampled groups and increased with age; not associated with hearing thresholds in this sample. Observational, not causal or an intervention. |
| [Ohlemiller et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19141317/) | Genetically matched pigmented and albino B6 mice, followed with cochlear-function and tissue measures | In mice older than 2 years, endocochlear-potential decline and marginal-cell loss were more common in albinos; animal evidence does not establish a human clearance effect. |
| [Neuromelanin organelle study, 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29900402/) | Human substantia-nigra tissue characterization | Long-lived autolysosome-like organelles contain undegraded proteins and lipids; no clinical clearance method demonstrated. |
| [Berro et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13408184/) | High-resolution elemental mapping of seven postmortem human locus-coeruleus tissues | Al, Ca, and Fe localized mainly to the pigment compartment; small observational sample, no clearance intervention. |
| [Zecca et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18988735/) | Chemical analysis of human-brain melanic pigments | Neuromelanins contain melanic, lipid, and peptide components and bind metals; composition differs by brain region. |
| [Human cardiac/hepatic/adrenal lipofuscin study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7206299/) | Human tissue study | Documents accumulation; not a clearance intervention. |
| [Cardiac lipofuscin study, 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0014482721001178) | Old rats | Reduced cardiac lipofuscin burden; no human clinical inference. |
| [Fibroblast lipofuscin study, 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37438887/) | Stressed human fibroblast cultures | Autophagy activation can accompany increased lipofuscin accumulation under processing failure. |
| [NIDDK hemochromatosis treatment](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/liver-disease/hemochromatosis/treatment) | Official clinical information | Excess iron depletion/chelation for disease; prior damage can persist. |
| [Ranganath et al., pigment regression, 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32904992/) | Human observational alkaptonuria cohort and ear-cartilage biopsies | Partial measured regression; not full reversal or evidence about normal iris melanin. |
| [SONIA 2, 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32822600/) | Randomized human alkaptonuria trial | Precursor lowering and slower disease progression. |
| [FDA Harliku approval](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/opdlisting/oopd/detailedIndex.cfm?cfgridkey=942723) | Official U.S. approval record, June 10, 2025 | Reduction of urinary homogentisic acid in adult alkaptonuria. |
| [NIH Recognizing Jaundice](https://newsinhealth.nih.gov/2022/10/recognizing-jaundice) | Official health information | Bilirubin-related color and cause-dependent medical management. |
| [NHLBI hemoglobin and red cells](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/anemia/causes) | Official physiological information | Blood-cell functions and hemoglobin; vascular color is a separate category from stored melanin. |
| [Hütter et al., 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17376148/) | Human vastus-lateralis muscle fibres from young and older donors | Lipofuscin accumulation was identified as an aging-associated marker; the study did not test selective pigment clearance. |
| [Chung et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19587090/) | Cross-sectional study of 25 healthy adults; HPLC of abdominal, buttock, and thigh adipose biopsies | Dietary carotenoids were stored in adipose tissue with site-dependent concentrations; not melanin and not a tissue-selective removal study. |
| [Ikeda et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34432824/) | Human adipose-derived stem-cell preparations studied in culture; melanocyte-marker assays and a 3D epidermal model | Cultured cells expressed melanocyte markers and generated melanin after differentiation and UVB exposure in the epidermal model; does not show a native melanin store in adipose tissue or clearance. |
| [Battyani et al., 1993](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7511806/) | RT-PCR survey of normal human organ samples | Tyrosinase transcripts detected in several organs, but expressing cells were not identified and tissue melanin was not measured; marker signal alone does not establish a pigment-bearing compartment. |
| [Laeng et al., 1988](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3282446/) | Human undecalcified iliac and vertebral bone specimens | Stainable bone-surface iron was uncommon in routine biopsies and more frequent in verified hemochromatosis; disease-associated iron, not a cosmetic target. |
| [Achilles tendon ochronosis case report, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39027761/) | Human alkaptonuria case report | Dark ochronotic pigment documented in a ruptured Achilles tendon; single case, and no tendon-specific pigment-reversal endpoint. |
| [Sepehri et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28486229/) | Mouse biodistribution study; black-ink n=10, red-ink n=10, controls n=5; one-year follow-up | Tattoo particles were identified in hepatic Kupffer cells; no particles were detected by light microscopy in other sampled internal organs. Animal distribution result, not human excretion or clearance. |
| [Capucetti et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2510392122) | Mouse tattooing and vaccination model; human and murine monocyte-derived macrophage culture; human lymph-node observations | Ink uptake induced macrophage apoptosis in vitro and persistent node inflammation in mice; vaccine outcomes were tested in mice. Does not establish clinical immune effects in tattooed people or apply directly to melanin. |
| [Kluger, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2611211123) | PNAS scientific letter commenting on the mouse tattooing study | Critiques exposure scale and translation to usual tattoo practice; interpret as a model-limit discussion, not a new experiment. |
| [Gonzalez, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2613440123) | PNAS author reply | Reiterates mechanistic scope, notes human-cell and lymph-node observations, and calls for further clinical study; does not resolve clinical translation. |
| [Cnop et al., 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19855953/) | Human pancreatic islet beta cells from donors aged 1–81 years; electron-microscopy morphometry and 3D modeling; non-diabetic n=45, type 2 diabetes n=10 | Lipofuscin proportion correlated with age and supported long beta-cell lifespan; no clearance or intervention tested. |
| [A Patient with Unexpected Golden Brown Tubular Staining](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11093538/) | Human kidney biopsy with histochemical and ultrastructural characterization | Incidental proximal-tubule lipofuscin in one biopsy; a case report, not a prevalence or clearance study. |
| [Weir et al., 1985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4031104/) | Ferritin and hemosiderin isolated from fresh-frozen human spleens removed from patients with transfusion-related secondary iron overload | Characterized iron-storage material by electron microscopy; not a selective tissue-clearance intervention. |
| [Primary nodal anthracosis case series](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3920429/) | Five human mediastinal lymph-node cases sampled by endobronchial ultrasound-guided aspiration | Carbon-containing particulate pigment in macrophages; diagnostic case series, not an endogenous pigment or clearance study. |

## Unassessed compartments

Evidence summaries now cover cochlear and meningeal melanin, cardiac melanocyte-like cells, esophageal melanocytosis, nasal and laryngeal mucosa, vulvar/cervical/anorectal findings, muscle lipofuscin, adipose carotenoids, candidate melanocyte-lineage signals in organ samples, cultured adipose-derived melanocyte progenitors, bone-associated iron in overload, tendon ochronosis, pancreatic beta-cell lipofuscin, a single proximal-tubule lipofuscin finding, splenic iron stores in secondary overload, particulate anthracosis in airways and draining lymph nodes, and colon melanosis-coli natural history. These are distinct deposits with different evidence levels; the colon reports show cause-associated endoscopic appearance resolution but do not measure pigment mass or export. None establishes a general tissue-selective cosmetic clearance capability. Dedicated assessment remains for many tissues and mucosal sites. A source in one tissue does not fill the gaps. Future entries should state the chemical substrate, cell type, model, clinical indication, actual pigment endpoint, functional endpoint, and reversibility evidence.
