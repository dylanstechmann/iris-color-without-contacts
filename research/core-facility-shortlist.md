# Core-facility / CRO shortlist: candidate execution sites for the paired exposure/withdrawal study

*November 9, 2026. Personal hobby and learning-project planning document; AI-assisted drafting, owner-reviewed. This shortlist identifies real facilities whose public capabilities overlap the four requirements of the [core-facility quote-request package](core-facility-quote-request.md). Claims are tied to URLs and marked CONFIRMED or VERIFY per the evidence-rules below. No quote requests have been sent; contact and verification are owner actions.*

---

## Methodology

**Search date:** 2026-11-09.  
**Search scope:** Web searches for academic core facilities and non-clinical CROs that combine (a) human melanocyte/melanoma cell culture or 3D pigmented skin equivalents, (b) stable-isotope (heavy-tyrosine or amino-acid) tracing experience, (c) LC–MS/MS analytical capability for both small-molecule intracellular quantification and melanin-marker/isotope-ratio work, and (d) explicit willingness to accept external/custom projects from non-academic or individual sponsors under a fee-for-service or collaboration agreement.  
**Evidence rule:** A capability is marked **CONFIRMED** only when a public page (facility website, iLab listing, or institutional core-directory entry) explicitly states it. It is marked **VERIFY** when the capability is plausible given related services but not explicitly claimed on a public page. Every claim is followed by a URL. No invented capabilities or contact names are included.  
**Disclaimer:** This document is planning information only. Public pages may be out of date, and a capability listed here does not guarantee current availability, pricing within the Phase 1/Phase 2 envelopes, or acceptance of this specific project. The owner must contact each facility directly and obtain a written quote before any commitment.

### 1. Weill Cornell Medicine — Proteomics and Metabolomics Core Facility

- **Institution:** Weill Cornell Medicine, Meyer Cancer Center  
- **Location:** New York, NY, USA  
- **Contact evidence:** iLab external-services portal — https://wcmc.corefacilities.org/service_center/show_external/3474  

| # | Required capability | Status | Evidence |
|---|---------------------|--------|----------|
| 1 | Human melanocyte/melanoma culture + 3D skin | **VERIFY** | The Core is an analytical mass-spectrometry facility, not a cell-culture production unit. The same institution hosts the Zippin laboratory, which has published heavy-tyrosine melanin fate-tracing work in human melanocytes and 3D skin models (Chen et al., 2021, *J Invest Dermatol*; Dolinska et al., 2026, *JCI Insight*), but the Core itself does not state that it performs or houses melanocyte culture. https://vivo.weill.cornell.edu/display/pubid33549605 |
| 2 | Stable-isotope tracing experience | **CONFIRMED** | Services page lists "Polar metabolite isotope tracing" among core offerings. https://gradschool.weill.cornell.edu/metabolomicsproteomics |
| 3 | LC–MS/MS small-molecule + melanin-marker work | **CONFIRMED** | Instrumentation includes Orbitrap Fusion Lumos and Q Exactive / Vanquish UPLC; services include targeted and untargeted metabolomics suitable for small-molecule intracellular quantification. https://wcmc.corefacilities.org/service_center/show_external/3474 |
| 4 | External / non-academic sponsor acceptance | **CONFIRMED** | Explicitly provides services "to WCM users as well as external users from outside academic institutions and commercial enterprises." External users pay via purchase order per iLab workflow. https://wcmc.corefacilities.org/service_center/show_external/3474 |

- **Pigment / melanocyte-specific bonus:** Institutional precedent. The Zippin lab at Weill Cornell developed and published the exact heavy-tyrosine LC–MS/MS melanin fate-tracing assay referenced in the RFQ (Chen et al., 2021). That does not confirm the Core itself has run melanocyte samples, but it indicates the method is institutionally familiar.
- **Sponsor / external-project policy:** Service agreement available for industrial users; iLab quote workflow for external users. Terms noted as non-negotiable for industrial agreements — verify whether an individual sponsor can sign under the same framework. https://wcmc.corefacilities.org/service_center/show_external/3474

## Cross-cutting assessment and hardest gaps

- **Capabilities that are easiest to confirm:** LC–MS/MS analytical capability and external-user acceptance. Every candidate on this shortlist has both explicitly stated on a public page.
- **Capabilities that are hardest to confirm:**
  1. **Human melanocyte/melanoma cell culture and 3D pigmented skin equivalents** — None of the six mass-spectrometry cores explicitly claims in-house melanocyte culture or 3D skin production. These are analytical cores that historically expect the investigator to supply cells or lysates. A facility that both cultures melanocytes *and* runs the mass-spectrometry/isotope-tracing pipeline under one roof would likely be a skin-biology CRO with an integrated analytics arm (e.g., Straticell, Genoskin, or Phenion with a partnered MS lab), but no such integrated CRO publicly advertises stable-isotope tracing of melanin. The most honest path is to treat melanocyte/3D-skin culture as a **VERIFY** item for every candidate and ask each facility whether it can subcontract or partner with an internal cell-culture/skin-engineering group.
  2. **Heavy-tyrosine melanin isotope-ratio LC–MS/MS** — Only Weill Cornell has an explicit institutional publication matching this exact assay (Chen et al., 2021). The other cores confirm general stable-isotope tracing (amino acids, central carbon metabolism, redox) but do not explicitly list heavy-tyrosine incorporation into melanin polymers. That method would need method-development time (consistent with the Phase 1 Gate 1 budget envelope).

- **Suggested owner action:** Send the RFQ to 2–3 of the CONFIRMED-external-user facilities above, explicitly stating that cell-culture execution and 3D tissue supply may need to be arranged via subcontract or institutional partner if the core cannot provide them directly. Ask each facility to confirm (a) whether it has ever run the Chen/Zippin heavy-tyrosine melanin protocol or similar, and (b) whether it can quote Gate 1 (old-pigment pre-labeling) and Gate 2 (intracellular compound validation) as separate, bounded first-stage deliverables.
