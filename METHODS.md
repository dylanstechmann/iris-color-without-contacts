# Methods and source boundaries

The [analysis methods](analysis/README.md) document the October 17, 2026 computational exploration, reproducible commands, input/output receipts and checks.

The owner's repositories are personal hobby and learning projects. This iteration was performed by the AI agent using the existing Regen Workbench and Research Desk. It does not imply the owner personally ran or reviewed every calculation.

The work consists of analytical and numerical hypothetical models, PubChem identity retrieval, RDKit descriptors/fingerprint comparisons, UniProt/structure metadata checks, source-specific pigment-binding/release benchmarks, an assay-provenance audit, selected public-workbook extraction, and a fixed-panel reanalysis of an existing human iris single-nucleus dataset. The exposure model separates free drug, binding, membrane escape and pigment feedback; its parameters are assumptions. Structural similarity does not supply missing candidate affinities. [Source accessions](data/ACCESSIONS.tsv) identify the inputs.

The October 26, 2026 [purple and orange sidequest](research/sidequest-purple-orange-eyes.md) adds a literature search for measured optical inputs and a toy CIELAB hue calculation, both run by the AI agent. The calculation uses the CIE 1931 2° observer and illuminant D65 data tables from the International Commission on Illumination (CC BY-SA 4.0), copied unmodified into [analysis/cie-tables](analysis/cie-tables/README.md), and one wavelength exponent taken from a published melanosome absorption law. Only those inputs are sourced; the iris spectra, stroma scattering and back return are assumptions that the calculation sweeps or calibrates.

Results distinguish logical consequences of assumptions, observations in public data, and reports from outside experiments. No clinical efficacy, binding affinity, ocular delivery, safe cosmetic intervention or reversible color cycle was established.
