# Eye color without contacts

A private learning and research project about whether a healthy adult can change iris color without wearing contact lenses.

## Current answer

DNA editing is not an established or approved cosmetic way to change eye color. Iris color depends on melanin and variation across multiple genes; the genetics that explain natural differences do not provide a safe, predictable method for editing an adult eye. No self-experimentation or human gene-editing work is part of this project.

In the United States, the FDA-approved CustomFlex artificial iris is indicated for full or partial aniridia and related iris defects. Its manufacturer says it is not intended for cosmetic eye-color changes in a normal eye. The project will treat permanent procedures on healthy eyes as a medical-risk question, not a recommendation.

For a temporary change in appearance without touching the eye, this project may consider nonmedical options such as lighting, eyewear styling, or photo previews. These do not change iris pigment.

## Project goals

- Summarize what is known about iris pigmentation and eye-color genetics.
- Separate genetic association from an actual treatment that can safely change an adult iris.
- Track clinical evidence and regulatory indications for proposed interventions.
- Clearly label what is established, experimental, speculative, or unsupported.

## Safety and scope

This is an educational literature review, not medical advice. It will not provide instructions, sequences, protocols, delivery methods, or sourcing for editing human DNA or eye tissue; nor instructions for eye surgery, lasers, or self-experimentation. Anyone considering an eye procedure should discuss it with a licensed ophthalmologist who is independent of the seller or clinic.

- [FDA: CustomFlex Artificial Iris PMA](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfpma/pma.cfm?id=P170039) — U.S. indication covers aniridia and related iris defects.
- [HumanOptics: CustomFlex Artificial Iris](https://www.humanoptics.com/en/surgeons/artificialiris/artificialiris-customflex/) — manufacturer states that the device is not intended for cosmetic eye-color changes.
