# Reversible cosmetic iris pigmentation without contacts or surgery

A private, theoretical research project on **increasing and decreasing iris melanin for cosmetic purposes**, with the example goal:

**Blue eyes → dark brown eyes → back to the original blue.**

The central question is whether a future nonsurgical system could provide controlled, bidirectional changes in visible iris pigmentation while preserving pigment cells, eye function, and vision. Reversibility and return to the person's baseline are core requirements.

A modulator need not change DNA. Pharmacologic and gene-regulatory biology are relevant to the literature review; the molecular implementation remains unspecified.

## Current assessment

Adult iris darkening has been observed as a side effect of some glaucoma medicines. It demonstrates a drug-associated appearance shift in susceptible eyes, not a controllable cosmetic color switch; small human morphometry links darkening with larger anterior-border melanin granules but does not show a rise in total granule counts or later clearance. [Cracknell et al., 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17292473/). Responses depend on the starting iris: current product information reports no observed change in uniformly blue eyes in the cited studies. [Xalatan product information](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/1616/smpc)

Direct human uveal-cell and donor-iris studies show that new melanin production can be modulated in experimental models. One small rabbit study reported lower final iris melanin when an inhibitor accompanied an inducing drug, but involved three animals in the relevant cohort, had a numerical discrepancy between the abstract and table, and did not measure pigment after withdrawal. These data support preclinical production modulation, not human pigment clearance or a reversible color cycle. [Drago et al., 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9986745/), [Lindsey et al., 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11405836/), [Sadiq et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9674870/)

The return direction is a separate problem. Human follow-up found no fading after stopping over two years, and one case documented persistence over five years. A separate periocular-skin case lightened after stopping, showing that pigment fate differs by tissue. Lowering new melanin production would not necessarily reduce pigment already present. U.S. labeling describes added iris pigmentation as likely permanent. [Two-year clinical report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9154289/), [five-year case](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10708238/), [periocular-skin case](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaophthalmology/fullarticle/265847), [U.S. prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f4e73059-5ba0-4d73-9ea1-09d8d654e844)

A rare disease counterexample shows that iris-stromal appearance can repigment: two previously reported BADI patients repigmented over four years, and a separate case report found that repigmentation had begun by year three but remained below the pre-disease appearance. A 2024 five-patient mixed BADI/BAIT series reported a glaucoma-related visual-field change in one BADI patient and a tube-shunt implant in one BAIT patient. BADI also involves pigment entering the anterior chamber and depositing in the drainage angle; its cause is unknown, and some patients experienced pressure elevation. These cases lack pigment-mass or destination measurements and do not demonstrate controlled, safe cosmetic reversal. [Tugal-Tutkun et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19545903/), [Atilgan et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6056785/), [Costa et al., 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11623184/)

The full blue → dark brown → blue sequence remains hypothetical. FDA currently states that there are no approved eye drops to change eye color. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/buying-using-medicine-safely/what-you-should-know-about-eye-drops)

New skin-imaging studies sharpen the measurement question: 3D multiphoton methods can estimate epidermal melanin density and its layer distribution in vivo. In a 20-person retinoid occlusion study, the measured density remained lower through the final day-32 scan, 20 days after exposure ended; that is a one-direction treatment response, not return to baseline. A 2025 melasma study likewise found lower layer-specific TPM scores among 53 completers, but had no untreated treatment-phase comparator. Neither study measured where pigment products went. [Tancrède-Bohin et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25220296/), [Ning et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2025.2790).

The broader pigment review suggests a shared **control framework**—identify the pigment and compartment, match processing to the substrate, account for breakdown products, preserve tissue function, and define a stopping/recovery endpoint—but no universal clearance mechanism. Human tracer work on bilirubin gives a narrow example of substrate-specific metabolism and measured excretion; it does not establish a route for clearing melanin or lipofuscin. Skin has the strongest current mechanistic lead: autophagy manipulation moved measured melanin up and down in ex-vivo human skin and skin substitutes. A small randomized human axillary-hyperpigmentation trial also found lower Fontana–Masson-positive epidermal area after niacinamide or desonide than placebo, without a detected melanocyte-count decrease; this was stain-based histology in an inflammatory condition, not chemical mass or export. No living-person washout/reversal test exists, and skin findings do not establish iris clearance. [Axillary biopsy trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23355788/) A historical vitiligo depigmentation label describes melanocyte destruction, frequently irreversible and irregular pigment loss, and lifelong skin sensitivity; an FDA review later recorded the product as no longer marketed. That is cell loss, not selective reversible pigment clearance. [Historical FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2003/08173slr015_benoquin_lbl.pdf), [FDA review](https://www.fda.gov/media/160999/download?attachment=). A separate mouse keratinocyte study degraded melanosomal proteins without reducing measured melanin even after two weeks, so organelle-marker loss is not proof that the pigment core was removed. A 2026 human-keratinocyte model found that KRT5/14 filaments, microtubules and plectin-1 position melanin-containing organelles above the nucleus, where they shield DNA from UVB; this is spatial-function evidence, not pigment clearance. [Benito-Martínez et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41398050/). Direct polymer breakdown and safe product export remain key gaps. A 2026 ex-vivo human-skin study found that UVA1 darkening coincided with eumelanin and pheomelanin marker changes, so chemical modification can coexist with a darker appearance. Human donor RPE studies also show age-associated melanin decline; intense visible light photobleached RPE melanosomes in human eyecups and formed hydrogen peroxide. This may involve photo-oxidation of a photoprotective pigment, and does not demonstrate controlled export or iris reversibility. [Feeney-Burns et al., 1984](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6698741/), [Schmidt and Peisch, 1986](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3721785/), [Sarna et al., 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12589778/). Superoxide degradation of isolated human retinal pigment epithelium melanosomes produced soluble fluorescent products; their destination and safety in living tissue remain unmeasured. [Ito et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms27093973), [Dontsov et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28941459/). Healthy people excrete melanin-pathway metabolites, and urinary studies measure eumelanin- and pheomelanin-related signals; some assays generate PTCA or AHP by chemically processing the urine. These data show normal output of pathway products but do not trace previously stored tissue pigment into urine. [Wirestrand et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2413694/), [Westerhof et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/1523-1747.ep12580422), [Wakamatsu et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16524432/). Rare melanuria in metastatic melanoma is a pathological high-burden event; one case reported renal melanin deposits and tubular injury. [Melanuria report](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMicm1810151), [renal case](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18572978/). [Marubashi and Fukuda, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10739166/). Gingiva has a separate topical-only human signal: a 73-person split-mouth trial reported a relative lightness difference versus placebo by week 4, but did not measure pigment mass/export, distinguish reduced synthesis from loss of stored pigment, or assess color after stopping. [Shimada et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19186973/). A 2026 human-keratinocyte model reported lower measured intracellular melanin after uptake of mouse melanoma-derived melanosomes, while leaving intact-tissue clearance, pigment disposition, and a pH inconsistency unresolved. [Zhao et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/jocd.70770). In a rat neuromelanin-overproduction model, TFEB lowered intracellular pigment and improved disease-model outcomes, but lysosomal exocytosis was observed in cells without an in-vivo destination or mass balance; extracellular neuromelanin can activate microglia and injure dopamine neurons in preclinical models. This brain-specific result does not establish clearance of peripheral or iris melanin. [Carballo-Carbajal et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-08858-y), [Zhang et al., 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19957214/), [Korzhevskii et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8419629/). A colon-specific example, melanosis coli, sometimes becomes invisible on follow-up colonoscopy after the inducing exposure ends; that appearance endpoint does not establish where the lipofuscin-like material went. [Tissue pigment atlas](research/tissue-pigment-atlas.md), [selective-clearance framework](research/selective-clearance-framework.md)

## Research scope

The atlas and clearance framework map selective pigment clearance across major body compartments. It distinguishes clinical treatment of specific disorders, human research, preclinical mechanisms, procedural comparisons, and unassessed tissues. This broader context supports the iris project; it does not establish one mechanism for every pigment or tissue.

The intended outcome is a cosmetic change in natural iris pigmentation without contact lenses or surgery. The research will examine:

- Increasing pigment synthesis and reducing the existing pigment burden as distinct endpoints.
- Molecular selectivity and selectivity for the intended iris tissue.
- Preservation of melanocytes, pupil function, vision, and other ocular tissues.
- Uniformity within and between eyes, onset, durability, and return to baseline.
- Whether repeated changes could occur without accumulating pigment or tissue damage.

This is literature review and conceptual analysis. The repo contains no treatment instructions or experimental administration plans. The cited medicines are evidence of pigment biology, not proposed cosmetic treatments.
