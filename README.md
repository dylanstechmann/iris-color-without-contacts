# Reversible cosmetic iris pigmentation without contacts or surgery

A private, theoretical research project on **increasing and decreasing iris melanin for cosmetic purposes**, with the example goal:

**Blue eyes → dark brown eyes → back to the original blue.**

The central question is whether a future nonsurgical system could provide controlled, bidirectional changes in visible iris pigmentation while preserving pigment cells, eye function, and vision. Reversibility and return to the person's baseline are core requirements.

A modulator need not change DNA. Pharmacologic and gene-regulatory biology are relevant to the literature review; the molecular implementation remains unspecified.

## Current assessment

Adult iris darkening has been observed as a side effect of some glaucoma medicines. It demonstrates a drug-associated appearance shift in susceptible eyes, not a controllable cosmetic color switch; small human morphometry links darkening with larger anterior-border melanin granules but does not show a rise in total granule counts or later clearance. [Cracknell et al., 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17292473/). Responses depend on the starting iris: current product information reports no observed change in uniformly blue eyes in the cited studies. [Xalatan product information](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/1616/smpc) A separate POAG surgical series found no latanoprost-associated increase in trabecular-meshwork pigmentation even though four treated specimens had documented iris darkening; it did not trace pigment to a final destination. [Cracknell et al., 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16297910/).

The literature also includes a photoablative laser procedure for iris lightening. One clinic reported 1,176 treated eyes, but its series was uncontrolled, did not measure pigment mass or fate, and did not test return to the original color; the stated nine-year program history is not nine years of follow-up for every eye. Independent case reports document severe glaucoma, maculopathy, high eye pressure, and corneal scarring with vision loss. The laser is a procedure, not a reversible biological modulator, and the case reports do not estimate complication rates. [Laser series](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8035100/), [2026 case report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41982480/).

Direct human uveal-cell and donor-iris studies show that new melanin production can be modulated in experimental models. One small rabbit study reported lower final iris melanin when an inhibitor accompanied an inducing drug, but involved three animals in the relevant cohort, had a numerical discrepancy between the abstract and table, and did not measure pigment after withdrawal. These data support preclinical production modulation, not human pigment clearance or a reversible color cycle. [Drago et al., 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9986745/), [Lindsey et al., 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11405836/), [Sadiq et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9674870/)

The return direction is a separate problem. Human follow-up found no fading after stopping over two years, and one case documented persistence over five years. A separate periocular-skin case lightened after stopping, showing that pigment fate differs by tissue. A slit-lamp-photographed single case also reported reversal of prostaglandin-associated conjunctival pigmentation after withdrawal; this ocular-surface result does not establish iris reversal. [Conjunctival case](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25967530/). Lowering new melanin production would not necessarily reduce pigment already present. U.S. labeling describes added iris pigmentation as likely permanent. [Two-year clinical report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9154289/), [five-year case](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10708238/), [periocular-skin case](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaophthalmology/fullarticle/265847), [U.S. prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f4e73059-5ba0-4d73-9ea1-09d8d654e844)

A rare disease counterexample shows that iris-stromal appearance can repigment: two previously reported BADI patients repigmented over four years, and a separate case report found that repigmentation had begun by year three but remained below the pre-disease appearance. A 2024 five-patient mixed BADI/BAIT series reported a glaucoma-related visual-field change in one BADI patient and a tube-shunt implant in one BAIT patient. BADI also involves pigment entering the anterior chamber and depositing in the drainage angle; its cause is unknown, and some patients experienced pressure elevation. These cases lack pigment-mass or destination measurements and do not demonstrate controlled, safe cosmetic reversal. [Tugal-Tutkun et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19545903/), [Atilgan et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6056785/), [Costa et al., 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11623184/)

The full blue → dark brown → blue sequence remains hypothetical. FDA currently states that there are no approved eye drops to change eye color. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/buying-using-medicine-safely/what-you-should-know-about-eye-drops)

New skin-imaging studies sharpen the measurement question: 3D multiphoton methods can estimate epidermal melanin density and its layer distribution in vivo. In a 20-person retinoid occlusion study, the measured density remained lower through the final day-32 scan, 20 days after exposure ended; that is a one-direction treatment response, not return to baseline. A 2025 melasma study likewise found lower layer-specific TPM scores among 53 completers, but had no untreated treatment-phase comparator. Neither study measured where pigment products went. [Tancrède-Bohin et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25220296/), [Ning et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2025.2790). A UV-B mouse RF study reported lower later skin staining with more lymphatic markers, but tyrosinase activity also fell and Results/Fig. 4 conflict with the abstract on lymph-node pigment direction; it did not trace pigment out of the node or body. [Kim et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules27020454). A 1984 guinea-pig tracer experiment also found greater loss of labeled melanin from dermis during contact dermatitis, but did not identify where it went; this is inflammation-associated model evidence, not a selective cosmetic method. [Takematsu and Seiji, 1984](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00511063). A mouse tattoo study also showed that ink can persist through local macrophage capture–release–recapture; in that model, dermal macrophages did not migrate to draining nodes, so node pigment alone does not show that intact melanophages moved there. [Baranska et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1084/jem.20171608). A 2021 report described improvement in two inflammatory-pigmentation cases after microneedle RF, but measured appearance only and had no controls or post-treatment return assessment. [Kim et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules26051297).

The broader pigment review suggests a shared **control framework**—identify the pigment and compartment, match processing to the substrate, account for breakdown products, preserve tissue function, and define a stopping/recovery endpoint—but no universal clearance mechanism. Human tracer work on bilirubin gives a narrow example of substrate-specific metabolism and measured excretion; it does not establish a route for clearing melanin or lipofuscin. Skin has the strongest current mechanistic lead: autophagy manipulation moved measured melanin up and down in ex-vivo human skin and skin substitutes. Two 2026 preclinical studies add post-transfer clearance signals: glabridin promoted autophagy-dependent degradation in keratinocytes, pigmented skin equivalents, and zebrafish; a Nelumbo extract/quercetin-3-O-glucuronide restored autophagy-linked clearance in UVB models and reduced pigmentation in a 3D skin organoid and guinea pigs. A solar-lentigo study linked ER stress to both increased synthesis and slower clearance. None demonstrates chemical product export or a human return-after-withdrawal cycle. [Nie et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42019358/), [Wen et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41581445/), [Hwang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00018-026-06389-6). A separate mouse-melanocyte cell study linked AHR–TFAP2A to MYO5A/MLPH melanosome transport and distribution, without measuring pigment loss or export. [Sung et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jid.2026.08.019). A 2026 vitiligo study linked laminin signaling to melanocyte state changes; pathway modulation partly restored pigmentation in models but did not demonstrate stored-pigment clearance or human cosmetic control. [Yang et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-72064-w). A small randomized human axillary-hyperpigmentation trial also found lower Fontana–Masson-positive epidermal area after niacinamide or desonide than placebo, without a detected melanocyte-count decrease; this was stain-based histology in an inflammatory condition, not chemical mass or export. No living-person washout/reversal test exists, and skin findings do not establish iris clearance. [Axillary biopsy trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23355788/) A historical vitiligo depigmentation label describes melanocyte destruction, frequently irreversible and irregular pigment loss, and lifelong skin sensitivity; an FDA review later recorded the product as no longer marketed. That is cell loss, not selective reversible pigment clearance. [Historical FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2003/08173slr015_benoquin_lbl.pdf), [FDA review](https://www.fda.gov/media/160999/download?attachment=). A separate mouse keratinocyte study degraded melanosomal proteins without reducing measured melanin even after two weeks, so organelle-marker loss is not proof that the pigment core was removed. A 2026 human-keratinocyte model found that KRT5/14 filaments, microtubules and plectin-1 position melanin-containing organelles above the nucleus, where they shield DNA from UVB; this is spatial-function evidence, not pigment clearance. [Benito-Martínez et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41398050/). A stable-isotope LC–MS method can measure newly synthesized melanin-pathway intermediates in live melanocyte models, while leaving the fate of stored pigment unresolved. [Chen et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33549605/). Primary human-cell models also show existing melanin moving from melanocytes to keratinocytes and to dermal fibroblasts/macrophages; cross-sectional ashy-dermatosis biopsies show pigment-bearing fibroblasts, making intercellular redistribution a documented possibility rather than proof of skin clearance. [Cabaço et al., 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9460155/), [Ando et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7461560/), [Hada et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/pcmr.70110). Direct polymer breakdown and safe product export remain key gaps. A 2026 ex-vivo human-skin study found that UVA1 darkening coincided with eumelanin and pheomelanin marker changes, so chemical modification can coexist with a darker appearance. Human donor RPE studies also show age-associated melanin decline; intense visible light photobleached RPE melanosomes in human eyecups and formed hydrogen peroxide. This may involve photo-oxidation of a photoprotective pigment, and does not demonstrate controlled export or iris reversibility. [Feeney-Burns et al., 1984](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6698741/), [Schmidt and Peisch, 1986](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3721785/), [Sarna et al., 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12589778/). Superoxide degradation of isolated human retinal pigment epithelium melanosomes produced soluble fluorescent products; their destination and safety in living tissue remain unmeasured. [Ito et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms27093973), [Dontsov et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28941459/). At the RPE, a 2023 pigmented Abca4−/− mouse study found that SIN-1 exposure was followed by lower measured areas for lipofuscin (~30%), melanolipofuscin (~50%), and melanin (~20%); RPE-like melanosomes were also observed in choriocapillaris. This is co-loss/possible export in a disease model, not selective melanin clearance, whole-eye mass balance, or evidence for iris or cross-tissue use. [Lyu et al., 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10194005/). RPE bisretinoid research has a separate, substrate-specific preclinical lead: β-cyclodextrins reduced selected bisretinoids in cultures and mouse eyecups, while acid-labile carriers promoted A2E excretion into ARPE-19 culture medium. The β-cyclodextrin findings do not demonstrate whole-eye/body disposition or a route for melanin; a separate retinal-explant study found cyclodextrin toxicity varied with derivative and concentration. [Nociari et al., 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3986126/), [Tamura et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29120644/), [Prajapati et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7967144/). Healthy people excrete melanin-pathway metabolites, and urinary studies measure eumelanin- and pheomelanin-related signals; some assays generate PTCA or AHP by chemically processing the urine. These data show normal output of pathway products but do not trace previously stored tissue pigment into urine. [Wirestrand et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2413694/), [Westerhof et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/1523-1747.ep12580422), [Wakamatsu et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16524432/). Rare melanuria in metastatic melanoma is a pathological high-burden event; one case reported renal melanin deposits and tubular injury. [Melanuria report](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMicm1810151), [renal case](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18572978/). [Marubashi and Fukuda, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10739166/). Gingiva has a separate topical-only human signal: a 73-person split-mouth trial reported a relative lightness difference versus placebo by week 4, but did not measure pigment mass/export, distinguish reduced synthesis from loss of stored pigment, or assess color after stopping. [Shimada et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19186973/). A 2025 split-mouth trial (20 participants) found that intragingival vitamin C injections and scalpel treatment had no significant difference on clinical pigmentation indices at six months, despite a stronger early change with scalpel; the injection side reported less pain/itch. It was small, retrospectively registered, and measured no chemical pigment mass, export, or return after stopping. [Al-Hajri et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13104-025-07476-w). A 2026 systematic review of microneedling plus ascorbic acid included seven studies but rated certainty low to very low; wound-healing and recurrence findings varied, and long-term durability or superiority remained unconfirmed. It predates the September 2 Dawar trial and synthesizes clinical outcomes rather than pigment mass or export. [Kusumawardani et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12903-026-09483-0). A 2023 primary-human-gingival-keratinocyte model loaded with *Sepia* melanin supports oral-product biocompatibility screening, but it does not model native pigment transfer or clearance. [Goenka, 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/oral3020021). A 2026 HCP-treated human-keratinocyte model reported lower cell-normalized intracellular melanin absorbance after uptake of mouse melanoma-derived melanosomes, but did not measure oxidized products or pigment export. Its Methods dose labels and figure/result concentrations do not reconcile, and its Results/abstract and Discussion give opposite pH directions. [Zhao et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/jocd.70770). In a rat neuromelanin-overproduction model, TFEB lowered intracellular pigment and improved disease-model outcomes, but lysosomal exocytosis was observed in cells without an in-vivo destination or mass balance; extracellular neuromelanin can activate microglia and injure dopamine neurons in preclinical models. This brain-specific result does not establish clearance of peripheral or iris melanin. [Carballo-Carbajal et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-08858-y), [Zhang et al., 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19957214/), [Korzhevskii et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8419629/). A colon-specific example, melanosis coli, sometimes becomes invisible on follow-up colonoscopy after the inducing exposure ends; that appearance endpoint does not establish where the lipofuscin-like material went. [Tissue pigment atlas](research/tissue-pigment-atlas.md), [selective-clearance framework](research/selective-clearance-framework.md)

A cochlear comparison helps separate lack of pigment from loss of pigment cells: albino mice without melanin had normal baseline hearing in one study, while mice lacking cochlear melanocytes had severe impairment; another mouse model showed hearing impairment despite strial intermediate cells being present. Noise-stress findings still leave a possible protective role for melanin. These are developmental and animal data, not evidence that removing pigment from a mature tissue is safe. [Motohashi et al., 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7852195/), [Kawaguchi, 1992](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1602357/), [Tan et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11762213/).

An ex-vivo porcine-skin study adds a caution for oxidative pigment-modification ideas: UV-triggered lipid peroxidation generated radicals and carbonyl intermediates proposed to excite melanin after exposure. It did not measure melanin loss, export, DNA injury, or living-human effects, so it is a safety-mechanism finding rather than evidence of clearance. [Pospíšil et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41088527/).

Cross-tissue gum evidence is mixed: two observational studies (2014, n=200; 2024, n=839) reported skin–gingiva associations, while two later cohorts (2025, n=261; 2026, n=100) did not. A small 2026 procedure trial likewise found no significant correlation between facial and gingival melanin-meter scores at follow-up. These studies use different populations and appearance measures; none validates facial complexion as a substitute for measuring gum pigmentation. [Ponnaiyan et al., 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24603930/), [Koppolu et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12903-024-04189-7), [Jouhar, 2025](https://doi.org/10.12669/pjms.41.5.11824), [Chahal et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41930043/), [Dawar et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jobcr.2026.101530). A separate 2026 gingival trial (30 participants) compared combined omega-3 injections plus oral supplementation with scalpel depigmentation; clinical scores improved, but the study had no component-only arms, used an oral-use product the authors said was not formulated for injection, and measured no pigment mass or export. Its registry first-submission date followed the listed study start, and the paper contains conflicting dose descriptions. [Hussien et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12903-026-08354-y), [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07067515](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07067515).
Hair is a separate follicle-control problem: a 2026 prospective oncology cohort associated checkpoint-inhibitor-containing regimens with greater hair-image darkening than chemotherapy alone, but most patients also received chemotherapy, the groups were not randomized, and no off-treatment return was measured. This supports pigment-production plasticity, not a cosmetic treatment. [Jiang et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42387269/).

## Research scope

The atlas and clearance framework map selective pigment clearance across major body compartments. It distinguishes clinical treatment of specific disorders, human research, preclinical mechanisms, procedural comparisons, and unassessed tissues. This broader context supports the iris project; it does not establish one mechanism for every pigment or tissue.

The intended outcome is a cosmetic change in natural iris pigmentation without contact lenses or surgery. The research will examine:

- Increasing pigment synthesis and reducing the existing pigment burden as distinct endpoints.
- Molecular selectivity and selectivity for the intended iris tissue.
- Preservation of melanocytes, pupil function, vision, and other ocular tissues.
- Uniformity within and between eyes, onset, durability, and return to baseline.
- Whether repeated changes could occur without accumulating pigment or tissue damage.

This is literature review and conceptual analysis. The repo contains no treatment instructions or experimental administration plans. The cited medicines are evidence of pigment biology, not proposed cosmetic treatments.
